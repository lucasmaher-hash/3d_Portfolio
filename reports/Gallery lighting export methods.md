# Preserve Cycles Lighting Through Controlled Baking

**Recommendation: keep Blender Cycles, bake the approved static diffuse appearance into HDR textures, and retain separate reflections and transparent materials for the moving walkthrough.** This offers the strongest practical route to preserving the approved room without rebuilding its look with browser lights. A more expensive baker alone will not correct the problems identified in the present export and display pipeline. The main improvements are preserving linear light, matching Blender’s actual colour transform, allocating UV space correctly, and avoiding duplicate lighting. Preserve option three and the exact current web bottle; create an independent fourth option at the proposed `/gallery-baked.html`. Before any full bake, prove the method on a small representative area with the rest of the scene still present for lighting. This report recommends that pilot; **no new bake, export, or browser implementation has been performed for this research**.

## The approved scene needs a new delivery pipeline

**Verified local findings:** the approved v05 source already uses the web-calibrated geometry and contains the revised Unify expression and intentional center-wall transition. The `CenterTopFalloff` material attribute darkens only the upper interior region, reaching a maximum 22% linear reduction and fading near the exit. That appearance belongs in the exported diffuse result. The old exporter additionally transforms scene height, light energy, and product positions; repeating those transformations on this source would change the approved lighting relationships. The source must therefore be copied into a separate export workspace rather than passed through the old exporter unchanged. These findings come from the local review scripts and settings, not from public documentation.

The existing exporter bakes diffuse colour, direct light, and indirect light into eight-bit images, then displays them through an emissive material. That representation is not inherently wrong: it can store the light leaving a matte surface. Its weaknesses are the image range and the later display corrections. The current third-version runtime also applies its own center-wall remapping and uses base Three.js AgX at exposure 1. Those are independent appearance changes, rather than a literal reproduction of the approved Blender pipeline. **Remove those corrections only in the new fourth runtime; leave the third option intact.**

For the fourth version, the proposed default is Cycles `DIFFUSE` baking with **Direct, Indirect, and Color enabled**, using the complete approved material shader and floating-point images. This stores outgoing diffuse radiance, including material colour, panel detail, light spill, shadows, and the center gradient. Display it as a baked diffuse term—an emission channel with black diffuse base is a practical starting representation—then add a separately verified specular contribution. It must receive no additional diffuse lighting or second albedo multiplication. Native baking exposes these contributions separately, making an explicit diffuse bake easier to reason about than a general Combined bake. ([Cycles baking](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/cycles/baking.html), [render-pass definitions](https://docs.blender.org/manual/id/4.5/render/layers/passes.html))

The main alternative is a colour-free illumination bake used as a Three.js `lightMap`, with material colour kept separate. This makes future material edits easier and can separate low-frequency lighting from fine surface detail. It also requires calibration: Three’s lightmap enters an irradiance calculation and is multiplied by the diffuse BRDF, while the precise normalization of the selected Cycles output must be checked with a white test patch. A map already containing albedo cannot simply be assigned there. Ordinary environment diffuse lighting must also be disabled on fully baked surfaces or ambient light is counted twice. For this already-approved fixed scene, **outgoing diffuse is the simpler first choice; illumination-only lightmaps are the fallback if the pilot demonstrates a clear advantage**. ([MeshStandardMaterial](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshStandardMaterial.html), [r184 lightmap shader](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r184/src/renderers/shaders/ShaderChunk/lights_fragment_maps.glsl.js), [r184 physical BRDF](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r184/src/renderers/shaders/ShaderChunk/lights_physical_pars_fragment.glsl.js))

## Match colour before spending more bake time

**Verified local settings:** the approved Blender renders use AgX, **Medium High Contrast**, and exposure **−0.8624965 EV**. That exposure is approximately a 0.55 linear multiplier. Three.js r184’s stock AgX shader implements a base AgX approximation with its optional look call disabled; it does not reproduce Blender’s Medium High Contrast look just because both menus say “AgX.” The installed Blender OCIO configuration and its dependent LUTs should be frozen with the export, rather than substituting an upstream configuration later. ([r184 AgX implementation](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r184/src/renderers/shaders/ShaderChunk/tonemapping_pars_fragment.glsl.js), [Blender OCIO configuration](https://github.com/blender/blender/blob/blender-v5.0-release/release/datafiles/colormanagement/config.ocio))

**Proposed quality path:** keep baked diffuse and reflected light in scene-linear values until composition, then apply the frozen Blender display processor—including the approved exposure and contrast look—once. A generated OCIO GPU transform or a validated logarithmic shaper plus 3D LUT can implement this. A plain bounded LUT without an HDR shaper is not sufficient evidence of correct highlight handling. Three’s built-in tone mapping must be disabled when this processor owns the transform, and final sRGB encoding must happen exactly once. Start by comparing a linear colour/exposure chart against the CPU OCIO output, then compare matched scene cameras. This integration remains an implementation task, not a proven match. ([OCIO Baker](https://opencolorio.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api/baker.html), [Three colour management](https://threejs.org/manual/pages/color-management.html))

Keep **scene-linear OpenEXR masters**, preferably half-float with lossless compression, outside the public web assets. They retain values above white and allow exposure and delivery encoding to change without rebaking. A 16-bit PNG offers more fixed-point precision but is not an HDR floating-point replacement. Avoid saving the Blender view transform into those masters. ([Blender colour management](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.5/render/color_management.html), [image formats](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.0/files/media/image_formats.html))

A useful cheaper diagnostic is to apply the approved display transform offline to the diffuse textures and show them unlit, with browser tone mapping bypassed. This can test whether matte walls and rim spill survive export. It cannot provide a coherent linear composition with live reflections and product highlights, so it is a fallback for matte architecture rather than the preferred complete renderer. If the unlit diagnostic matches but the hybrid does not, that isolates the problem to runtime composition or colour handling instead of demanding another expensive bake.

## Bake illumination while keeping reflections responsive

**A single RGB texture per surface point cannot encode every viewing direction.** Baking glossy highlights or bottle refraction into the architecture would freeze them onto surfaces as the visitor walks. Keep the static diffuse room lighting baked, with view-dependent reflection sampling for satin floor, ceiling, podiums, and reflective products. This satisfies the request to avoid actual room lights: reflection captures are not dynamic light sources. Any runtime product-light exception should be explicit and tested separately, not hidden inside room-wide brightness controls. ([r184 environment shader](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/r184/src/renderers/shaders/ShaderChunk/envmap_physical_pars_fragment.glsl.js), [physical-material documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshPhysicalMaterial.html))

The proposed first compromise is offline environment captures near the center and exhibit bays, filtered with PMREM and assigned locally. Begin at 256-pixel cube faces and increase only if inspection justifies it. Stock environment sampling does not account for the reflection ray’s position within a room, so reflected features can slide incorrectly during movement. Local captures reduce that error; custom parallax correction can improve it but curved rooms remain an approximation. A blurred planar floor reflection is a possible later desktop upgrade, with another scene render as its cost. SSR is a weaker first choice because it cannot recover geometry absent from the current screen buffers. Progressive real-time path tracing adds convergence and capability constraints during movement. None has a measured performance result for this gallery yet. ([PMREMGenerator](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/PMREMGenerator.html), [Reflector implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/r184/examples/jsm/objects/Reflector.js), [SSRPass](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/SSRPass.html), [Three GPU path tracer](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/gkjohnson/three-gpu-pathtracer/main/README.md))

**Preserve the web bottle exactly.** Its third-version nodes are `bottle body_Podest`, `label_Podest`, and `lid_Podest`. Inspection found transmission 1, IOR 1.45, roughness 0.03 on the body, and no volume extension. The dark Cycles shoulder is not evidence of broken geometry; different glass transport is an unresolved alternative explanation. Three transmission samples a rendered scene texture, unlike Cycles path tracing. Preserve its mesh accessors, indices, normals, UVs, transforms, texture bytes, samplers, materials, and extensions, initially by loading the immutable existing asset. Do not weld, requantize, recalculate normals, or re-export that bottle through Blender. Its appearance still needs visual validation under the fourth scene’s new reflections and display transform; identical asset bytes cannot promise identical pixels under different illumination. ([Transmission implementation](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r184/src/renderers/shaders/ShaderChunk/transmission_pars_fragment.glsl.js), [glTF transmission](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF/tree/main/extensions/2.0/Khronos/KHR_materials_transmission), [glTF volume](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF/tree/main/extensions/2.0/Khronos/KHR_materials_volume))

## A representative pilot should decide the final budget

**First, freeze the baseline.** The preservation tag is `gallery-third-preserved-2026-10-22` at commit `3d505f1`; the third gallery GLB SHA256 is `084ed362ae0ff47f774db25e9b47baa5fa756bd71548d8204a4e1260fdd37c41`. Use separate fourth-version assets and runtime, retaining the approved source and references. Bake only a representative outer wall, podium, adjacent floor, rim, ceiling recess, and center-wall patch covering the top transition and exit fade. Keep the complete scene active for bounce light and shadows. Compare native Cycles against baked-only Cycles first, then the browser at matching camera transforms, projection, and image size.

**Second, solve density and noise separately.** Retain full approved geometry. Parameterize curved walls by physical arc length and profile distance, give underside/rim islands real UV area, avoid unintended overlap, and keep bake UVs independent of product UVs. Starting proposals are roughly 64–96 texels/metre for broad light fields, higher where grooves demand it, and 1K podium maps. At 4K, trial 16–32-pixel bake margins with enough island separation for both dilation and filtering; inspect lower mips while walking. These are test budgets, not universal quality settings. Blender’s Adjacent Faces margin can reduce seam discontinuities. ([Bake margins](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/cycles/baking.html), [Blender 3.1 release notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/3.1/cycles/))

Start a pilot around 256 samples with adaptive threshold 0.01 and minimum 64, comparing raw and denoised results. Increase only if smooth gradients fail convergence. **Native bake denoising and adaptive sampling have been supported since Blender 3.0**; claims that all bakes require external denoising are outdated. Installed-version behaviour and guide alignment still need checking. Preserve approved bounce/world/clamp settings initially; extra AO, aggressive clamping, or Fast GI changes appearance and cannot be treated as harmless noise removal. More samples cannot repair UV distortion, clipped light values, or duplicate tone mapping. ([Blender 3.0 release notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/3.0/cycles/), [sampling settings](https://docs.blender.org/manual/id/5.0/render/cycles/render_settings/sampling.html), [light paths](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.4/render/cycles/render_settings/light_paths.html))

**Third, optimize delivery only after appearance passes.** Use uncompressed/half-float pilot maps first. Compare UASTC against ETC1S for LDR delivery, especially on subtle gradients and labels; do not recompress the preserved bottle. Standard glTF `KHR_texture_basisu` does not define portable HDR UASTC material textures. Pinned Three r184 KTX2Loader separately supports standalone HDR UASTC with device-dependent compressed formats or half-float fallback, so HDR-KTX2 needs an explicit loader, manifest, and device test. Small JPEG downloads do not imply small GPU memory: a 2048² RGBA8 map uses about 21.3 MiB with mipmaps, whereas half-float RGBA uses about 42.7 MiB. Those are format arithmetic, not measured gallery totals. ([KHR_texture_basisu](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KhronosGroup/glTF/main/extensions/2.0/Khronos/KHR_texture_basisu/README.md), [r184 KTX2Loader](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/r184/examples/jsm/loaders/KTX2Loader.js), [texture memory](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/r184/manual/en/textures.html))

## Changing bakers offers no demonstrated fidelity advantage

Marmoset Toolbag offers lighting bakes and explicitly identifies specular as camera-dependent. Unreal GPU Lightmass offers static lighting through an engine-specific DXR pipeline. Neither source establishes faithful transfer of this approved Cycles material/lighting setup into a Three.js walkthrough. Reauthoring there introduces another material and display conversion before solving the existing one. Directional lightmaps can be useful when runtime normals need lighting-direction information, but add storage and shader work; they do not encode full glossy reflection or transmission. For fixed architecture whose look is already approved, no measured need justifies that complexity. Keep these as targeted alternatives if the pilot exposes a reproducible limitation, not as an engine switch now. ([Toolbag map types](https://docs.marmoset.co/docs/map-types/), [GPU Lightmass](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/gpu-lightmass-global-illumination-in-unreal-engine))

**Implementation acceptance checklist:** option three’s asset hash remains unchanged; bottle semantic hashes and runtime material properties match the preserved source; center gradient, exit fade, rim illumination, panel detail, and Unify expression survive; matte browser regions agree with matched approved cameras without per-wall correction; reflections and bottle remain convincing from multiple walking positions; seams and compression hold up at lower mip levels; desktop and phone loading, texture allocation, and frame times are measured. Exact pixel identity across Cycles and rasterized reflections is not promised. Full-scene bake duration, final atlas count, and GPU budget remain unknown until the pilot supplies evidence.

## Conclusion

The decisive improvement is a verifiable chain from approved scene to linear bake to final display, with failure localized at each step. A successful small pilot provides a defensible sample/resolution budget and shows which moving-view compromises actually matter. Only then should the full fourth gallery be baked; the current third gallery remains the protected fallback throughout.
